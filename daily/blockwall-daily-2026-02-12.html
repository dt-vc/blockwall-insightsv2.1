Paxful To Pay $4M For Moving Funds Tied to Criminal Schemes

News • Regulation • Score: 7/10

Paxful Holdings Inc., a peer-to-peer virtual currency trading platform, pleaded guilty to conspiring to violate the Travel Act, operate an unlicensed money transmitting business, and breach Bank Secrecy Act anti-money laundering requirements, resulting in a $4 million criminal penalty reduced from $112.5 million due to its inability to pay. The platform knowingly facilitated over $500 million in suspicious transactions tied to fraud, extortion, romance scams, illegal prostitution, hacks by state actors, and sanctions-evading countries like Iran, North Korea, and Venezuela, while promoting weak controls to attract criminal users. FinCEN separately imposed a $3.5 million civil penalty, half credited against the DOJ fine, for failures including no compliance officer, delayed suspicious activity reports, and systemic non-compliance. For institutional investors, this underscores escalating U.S. regulatory scrutiny on crypto platforms' AML programs and the risks of associating with high-risk P2P exchanges lacking robust customer identification.
