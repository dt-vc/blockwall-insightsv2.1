Crypto industry reels as April sees highest number of hacks ever

DL News • Security • Score: 9/10

April saw a record 29 crypto hacks, the highest monthly total ever reported. The attacks caused a combined loss of $579 million, with the largest hits to the Drift exchange and Kelp DAO. Analysis of the incidents shows that most were traced to code bugs and single points of failure in decentralized finance protocols. The spike underscores heightened security risk for institutional exposure to DeFi assets.

Is Aave too Big to Fail?

Messari • DeFi • Score: 8/10

A recent rsETH exploit caused a $193 M loss on Aave, prompting concerns about the protocol’s resilience. The ecosystem responded quickly, with DeFi United contributing over $300 M to fully reimburse affected depositors. Paradigm Files Comment Letter on the OCC’s GENIUS Rulemaking

Paradigm • Regulation • Score: 5/10

Paradigm submitted a formal comment letter to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency regarding the agency’s proposed rule under the GENIUS Act. The firm argues that the rule would overly broaden the prohibition on yield‑generating activities for payment stablecoins, potentially stifling innovation. Paradigm recommends that the OCC include safe‑harbor provisions, reduce reporting burdens, and clarify the treatment of white‑label stablecoins and cross‑chain transfers to preserve market development.

US Senate Bans Members, Staff from Prediction Markets

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 6/10

The U.S. Senate passed a resolution that bars members of Congress and their staff from taking part in prediction markets. The action was prompted by recent insider‑trading concerns, including allegations that a special‑forces soldier used classified information to place bets on Polymarket and broader worries about wagering on geopolitical events. Prediction‑market platforms such as Polymarket and Kalshi welcomed the measure because they already prohibit participation by elected officials and their aides, and the ban underscores regulatory scrutiny that could affect the market’s compliance and growth prospects for institutional investors.

DeFi’s Lose-Lose Problem on Freezing Stolen Funds

Cointelegraph • DeFi • Score: 5/10

Arbitrum chose to freeze the stolen assets linked to the $293 million Kelp DAO exploit, prompting a sharp debate within the crypto community. Critics argue that the move undermines the core DeFi principle of immutability, while supporters contend that emergency governance tools are essential to protect large‑scale capital. The discussion includes perspectives from THORChain, the CEO of Stables, and independent security experts who weigh the trade‑offs between protocol integrity and rapid response. Institutional investors are watching the outcome closely because the decision sets a precedent for how decentralized platforms may intervene in fraud cases and manage systemic risk.
