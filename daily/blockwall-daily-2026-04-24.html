🔥 Key Developments (8)

North Korea stole $293m in crypto. Then it used a victim's products to launder the proceeds

DL News • Security • Score: 9/10

North Korean state‑backed hackers stole $293 million from the Kelp DAO by exploiting a vulnerability in the LayerZero cross‑chain messaging protocol. They then used the same LayerZero infrastructure to launder a portion of the stolen funds. The theft is part of a broader wave of on‑chain thefts that total $579 million in less than 20 days. It represents the first documented instance of a blockchain bridge serving both as the attack vector and a laundering channel, highlighting heightened risk for institutions that rely on cross‑chain interoperability.

Weekly Top Stories: Kelp Needs Help After $290m Exploit

Galaxy • DeFi • Score: 9/10

A $290 million exploit of KelpDAO’s LayerZero bridge drained rsETH from an Ethereum escrow, representing the largest DeFi hack recorded in 2026. The attacker transferred the stolen ETH off‑chain and swapped it for Bitcoin through THORChain, which precipitated a cascade of liquidity freezes across related protocols. The breach caused Aave’s total value locked to fall by roughly 45 percent and highlights heightened systemic risk in DeFi lending and collateral markets.

Bitcoin developer wants to hard fork the network — and give away Satoshi coins

DL News • Bitcoin • Score: 8/10

Long‑time Bitcoin developer Paul Sztorc has announced a hard fork of the Bitcoin network that he calls eCash. The eCash fork is designed to add drivechains, a layer‑2 scaling solution, to the Bitcoin protocol. The proposal also includes a plan to reallocate up to half of the coins originally mined by Satoshi Nakamoto to fund ongoing development. Institutional investors are watching the initiative closely because it could affect network consensus and the distribution of a significant portion of Bitcoin’s early supply.

DeFi United: United We Stand, Divided We Fall

Tiger Research • DeFi • Score: 9/10

A single‑node vulnerability in KelpDAO’s LayerZero bridge allowed $292 M of rsETH to be minted without collateral. The unbacked tokens were deposited on Aave where they were used to borrow $190 M, precipitating a $13 B total value‑locked collapse across DeFi protocols. In response, a self‑organized rescue coalition named DeFi United launched a fundraising effort targeting up to $100 M to compensate affected participants. As of the latest update the coalition has raised less than half of its goal, underscoring the need for coordinated capital support to stabilize the market.

Prediction Market Battle Gets Closer to Supreme Court

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 7/10

U.S. state gambling regulators have intensified legal challenges against prediction‑market platforms. In response, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission has filed lawsuits in three states, moving the regulatory clash nearer to the Supreme Court. The core issue is whether platforms such as Kalshi, Robinhood and Coinbase may lawfully offer contracts tied to sports events. The outcome will determine the balance of federal versus state authority over emerging prediction‑market products, a matter of significance for institutional investors evaluating compliance risk.

South Africa

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 6/10

South Africa’s Treasury has issued a draft Capital Flow Management Regulation for 2026 that targets cryptocurrency activities. The draft would obligate South African residents to declare any crypto assets they hold when departing the country and would grant enforcement officers the authority to inspect digital wallets and seed phrases. It also restricts cross‑border crypto transactions to only those conducted through authorised service providers. Industry participants, including MoneyBadger founder Carel van Wyk, have criticised the proposal as overly restrictive for the crypto sector.
