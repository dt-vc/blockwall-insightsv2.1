U.S. CFTC opens crypto 'perp' door with first approval at regulated firm

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 9/10

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission approved the first bitcoin perpetual futures contract on a regulated U.S. exchange. The approval authorizes Kalshi to list the BTCPERP product and permits a Coinbase affiliate to direct U.S. customers to global options and perpetual contracts. This creates a regulatory framework for crypto derivatives in the United States and is seen as a significant step toward broader institutional adoption.

Samsung is buying a $408 million stake in South Korea’s biggest crypto exchange

CoinDesk • Investment • Score: 7/10

Samsung affiliates are purchasing a 4% equity stake in Dunamu, the company that operates Upbit, South Korea’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, for $408 million. The transaction lowers the shareholding of Kakao, which had disposed of roughly $1.5 billion of equity in the platform over the preceding month. By acquiring a material position in the exchange, Samsung signals a continued strategic focus on the digital asset ecosystem. The move may influence competitive dynamics among South Korean tech conglomerates and could be relevant for institutional investors monitoring exposure to crypto infrastructure.

CME ends bitcoin weekend gaps with launch of 24/7 futures trading from Friday

CoinDesk • Infrastructure • Score: 7/10

CME Group has launched 24/7 trading of Bitcoin futures and options on its Globex platform, ending the traditional weekend market closure that produced the “CME gap.” The new schedule begins on Friday and removes the overnight price discontinuity that institutional hedgers previously had to manage. Continuous trading allows market participants to hedge Bitcoin exposure at any time without waiting for the market to reopen. CME will still observe a short maintenance window on Saturdays to perform system updates.

Wall Street’s trillion-dollar dilemma: Why AI-powered hackers are keeping big banks off the blockchain

CoinDesk • Security • Score: 7/10

CertiK’s chief executive has warned that AI‑driven hacks have turned April into the worst month for decentralized finance security in four years, with attacks recorded on 27 of the 30 days. The sector has suffered high‑profile exploits such as a $1.46 billion breach of Bybit and an estimated $600 million loss across Drift Protocol and Kelp Dao. These incidents are prompting Wall Street’s major banks to pause plans to allocate trillions of dollars to on‑chain assets. Institutional investors are therefore reassessing the risk‑return profile of blockchain exposure until robust defensive measures against AI‑enhanced threats are demonstrated.

UniCredit warns Europe may struggle to contain crypto-bank crisis under MiCA rules

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 6/10

UniCredit has cautioned that the European Union’s deposit‑insurance scheme may not be robust enough to absorb a shock from a crypto‑bank under the Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) regulatory framework. The bank points out that the EU framework falls short of the broader coverage offered by U.S. deposit‑insurance protections. This gap could expose large stablecoin reserve accounts to heightened risk if a crypto‑bank failure occurs, raising concerns for investors and regulators about systemic stability.

VanEck’s tokenized VBILL US Treasury fund can now be used as collateral on Euler

The Block • Tokenization • Score: 6/10

VanEck’s tokenized VBILL US Treasury fund has been added to the roster of assets that Euler accepts as collateral. This inclusion enables token holders to pledge the digital Treasury security to obtain loans on Euler’s decentralized lending platform. By allowing a regulated fixed‑income product to be used in DeFi, the integration creates a bridge between traditional sovereign‑bond exposure and blockchain‑based financing. Institutional investors can therefore leverage their Treasury positions for liquidity while maintaining exposure to a low‑risk asset class.

Grayscale IPO delayed as crypto firms reassess public market plans

CoinDesk • Enterprise • Score: 6/10

Grayscale, a leading crypto asset manager, has postponed its planned U.S. initial public offering until at least the fourth quarter of 2026. The company cited weak market conditions and low investor demand as the primary reasons for the delay. The postponement is part of a broader cooling in the crypto IPO market, with peers such as Payward, ConsenSys and Ledger also pushing back their listings, signaling heightened caution among crypto firms about accessing public capital.

The outage underscores reliability risks for developers and institutional investors who depend on Sui’s high-performance infrastructure.
