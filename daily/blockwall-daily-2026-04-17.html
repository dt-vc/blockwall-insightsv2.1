Drift degen sues Circle, alleging stablecoin giant ‘did nothing’ during $295m hack

DL News • Security • Score: 9/10

A class‑action lawsuit has been filed by the DeFi platform Drift against Circle, alleging that the stablecoin issuer failed to prevent a hack that resulted in the loss of $295 million. The suit contends that Circle’s USDC and its cross‑chain transfer protocol enabled the attackers, who are linked to North Korea, to move the stolen assets from Solana to Ethereum and convert them into Ether. Circle has responded that it can only intervene in such matters when directed by a court order. Institutional investors are watching the case because it raises questions about the liability and risk management of stablecoin issuers and cross‑chain infrastructure in the broader crypto ecosystem.

Weekly Top Stories: Tether bails out Drift in bid for Solana market share

Galaxy • DeFi • Score: 9/10

Tether is committing up to $147.5 million to stabilize the Solana‑based Drift Protocol after it suffered a $295.7 million exploit. The rescue package comprises a $100 million revenue‑linked credit facility, an ecosystem grant and market‑maker loans aimed at restoring liquidity. As part of the plan, Drift will transition its settlement layer from USDC to USDT, a move that could enhance Tether’s presence in the Solana ecosystem and support broader market share gains.

Payward To Acquire CFTC-Regulated Crypto Derivatives Platform Bitnomial

Cointelegraph • Infrastructure • Score: 8/10

Payward, the parent company of the Kraken exchange, has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Bitnomial. Bitnomial is a U.S. crypto derivatives platform that holds a full CFTC license. The transaction values Bitnomial’s equity at $20 billion and provides Payward with native infrastructure for spot margin, perpetual futures and options trading for U.S. clients. The deal also broadens Payward’s API‑based crypto services, giving institutional investors a regulated pathway to access a wider range of derivative products in the United States.

Russia Introduces Bill To Criminalize Unregistered Crypto Services

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 7/10

Russia's State Duma has introduced a bill that would criminalize the operation of unregistered cryptocurrency services. The draft legislation provides for penalties of up to five years in prison and fines for entities that fail to register. It is designed to strengthen anti‑money‑laundering and know‑your‑customer controls over digital asset providers. If enacted, exchanges and other crypto service providers would need to obtain official licensing or cease operating in the Russian market.

Neo Co-Founder Proposes $461M Overhaul to End ‘Trust Me’ Governance

Cointelegraph • Enterprise • Score: 7/10
