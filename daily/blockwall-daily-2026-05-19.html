🔥 Key Developments (12)

Echo Protocol suffers $76 million exploit in eBTC minting attack on Monad

CoinDesk • Security • Score: 9/10

Echo Protocol, a Bitcoin‑focused DeFi platform on the Monad blockchain, suffered a major exploit that resulted in the unauthorized minting of roughly 1,000 eBTC tokens valued at about $77 million, representing an estimated $76 million loss. The attacker leveraged a compromised admin key to borrow $3.45 million in WBTC and subsequently laundered the proceeds through Tornado Cash. After regaining control of the protocol, the team burned the remaining illicit tokens, underscoring admin‑key and cross‑chain lending risks that institutional investors must evaluate.

The SEC wants to let newly public companies raise cash instantly in its biggest rule change in decades

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 8/10

The SEC has proposed its most extensive overhaul of IPO and public‑company rules in more than two decades. The plan would allow newly listed firms to file shelf registrations immediately and would remove the $75 million public‑float threshold. By cutting compliance costs the changes are intended to give mid‑size crypto companies faster and cheaper access to capital markets. Institutional investors could benefit from a broader range of newly public crypto issuers and more efficient fundraising opportunities.

Block by Block: How the rsETH Exploit Rattled Aave Lending Markets

Galaxy • Security • Score: 8/10

The rsETH exploit in mid‑April caused the largest shock to Aave V3 lending markets, freezing both stablecoin and WETH pools and driving utilization close to 100%. Within the first six hours after the exploit, $1.8 billion of stablecoins withdrew from the platform, with USDT and RLUSD experiencing the most significant outflows. The event highlights systemic risk in DeFi lending protocols and underscores the need for robust risk management for institutional participants.

Despite Trump’s pledge, a CBDC is being explored behind closed doors, says former CTFC chair

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 7/10

The United States is exploring a central bank digital currency in private despite President Trump’s public opposition, according to former CFTC chairman Timothy Massad. Massad said the U.S. is participating in the Bank for International Settlements’ Project Agora, a multilateral research initiative on wholesale CBDC architectures. He warned that a government‑backed digital dollar is inevitable as global tokenisation accelerates, which could reshape settlement, liquidity and cross‑border payment frameworks.

Former BNY exec Anthony Moro bets tokenization will remake Wall Street

CoinDesk • Tokenization • Score: 7/10

Former BNY Mellon executive Anthony Moro has launched NUWA, an Ethereum‑based, chain‑agnostic marketplace that moves tokenized home‑equity lines of credit and Treasuries onto decentralized finance. The platform aggregates roughly $19 billion of these tokenized assets and partners with Figure Technologies and Animoca Brands to provide infrastructure and market access. By issuing a yield‑bearing stablecoin that is structured as a registered security, NUWA aims to create a regulated bridge between traditional finance and crypto, a development that could reshape Wall Street’s approach to digital assets.

Even a mountain of T-bills won't save Tether and Circle from a sudden liquidity crisis, expert says

CoinDesk • Stablecoins • Score: 6/10

Union Investment’s head of digital assets, Christoph Hock, said that USDT and USDC are not true stablecoins because their reserves include gold and bitcoin, which he considers speculative assets. He pointed to recent de‑pegging episodes, including a 13% drop in USDC’s value, as a sign of liquidity risk that could affect institutional portfolios. The comments come as European regulators tighten oversight of private stablecoins, raising concerns that even large holdings of Treasury bills may not prevent a sudden liquidity crisis for the issuers.
