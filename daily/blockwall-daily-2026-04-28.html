Aave-led 'DeFi United' campaign raises $300m to address Kelp DAO breach

DL News • DeFi • Score: 9/10

Aave is leading the DeFi United campaign that has secured more than $300 million to compensate victims of the Kelp DAO/LayerZero breach that resulted in a $293 million loss. The recovery fund is being populated by contributions of Ether and stablecoins from a range of major DAOs, crypto enterprises and prominent individuals, reflecting broad sector support. This coordinated response underscores the growing capacity of decentralized‑finance participants to mobilize capital for risk mitigation and reinforces confidence among institutional investors in the ecosystem’s resilience.

A digital shekel is here: Israel approves its first-ever regulated stablecoin

CoinDesk • Stablecoins • Score: 7/10

Israel's Capital Market Authority has approved BILS, the country's first regulated stablecoin that is pegged to the Israeli shekel. The token is issued by Bits of Gold in partnership with the Solana blockchain, with custody services provided by Fireblocks and audit oversight from EY. This approval introduces a locally denominated digital asset and diversifies the stablecoin market beyond the dominant USD‑pegged offerings, which may be of interest to institutional investors seeking exposure to Israeli currency and regulated crypto infrastructure.

Aave publishes plan to plug $246m hole left by Kelp DAO attackers

DL News • DeFi • Score: 7/10

Aave has released a detailed plan to address the $246 million shortfall created by the Kelp DAO attack. The plan is coordinated with Compound and the DeFi United coalition to fully back rsETH and eliminate the bad debt. It calls for converting pledged ETH into rsETH and using governance votes to lower oracle prices so that controlled liquidations can be executed. Successful implementation would return the stolen assets to the users affected by the hack and restore confidence in the protocol’s risk management.

AML Fines Eclipse SEC Cases as Top Crypto Risk: Report

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 6/10

A new report shows that anti‑money‑laundering penalties for crypto firms have risen sharply, with sanctions‑related volume increasing more than 400 percent year over year. The report notes that these AML fines now outweigh the number of SEC enforcement actions, positioning regulatory risk as the top concern for the sector. This enforcement trend is occurring alongside tighter stablecoin regulations being applied by regulators. At the same time, the Basel Committee is preparing prudential standards that would require near‑full capital charges on major crypto assets such as Bitcoin and Ether, adding further capital cost considerations for institutional investors.

Bitmine buys $294m more Ethereum ahead of Clarity Act ‘markup in May’

DL News • Ethereum • Score: 6/10

Bitmine increased its Ether position by $294 million, raising its total exposure to about $12 billion, which represents roughly 4.2 % of the total Ether supply. The timing aligns with the U.S. Senate’s upcoming markup of the Clarity Act, a legislative step aimed at establishing a federal digital‑asset framework. The transaction is supported by a consortium of prominent investors including ARK, Founders Fund, Pantera, Kraken, Galaxy Digital and Bill Miller, underscoring institutional confidence in the asset ahead of potential regulatory clarity.

Japan Requests Real Estate and Crypto Firms Tighten AML Checks on Property Deals

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 5/10

Japan’s Financial Services Agency has instructed real‑estate agents and cryptocurrency firms to apply bank‑style anti‑money‑laundering (AML) checks to property transactions that involve crypto assets. The guidance classifies the conversion of crypto to fiat currency as a regulated exchange activity and requires reporting of large foreign crypto inflows that exceed set thresholds. The move aims to tighten oversight of cross‑border crypto flows in the real‑estate market, a development that institutional investors should monitor as it may affect compliance costs and transaction structuring.

Solana Clients Introduce Post-Quantum Solution Falcon

Cointelegraph • Infrastructure • Score: 5/10

Solana validator clients Anza and Firedancer have released test implementations of the Falcon post‑quantum signature scheme to prepare the network for future quantum threats. Falcon‑512 is the smallest NIST‑approved post‑quantum signature and can be verified off‑chain, which helps preserve Solana’s high‑throughput performance. The integration demonstrates proactive security hardening in the Solana ecosystem and may be of interest to institutional investors monitoring blockchain resilience.
