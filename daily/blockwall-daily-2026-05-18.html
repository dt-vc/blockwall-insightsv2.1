🔥 Key Developments (10)

Aave restores ether borrowing limits after $230 million exploit

CoinDesk • DeFi • Score: 9/10

Aave has lifted emergency borrowing limits on wrapped ether (WETH) across six of its V3 deployments, restoring the loan‑to‑value ratios that were in place before the incident. The action follows a large rsETH exploit that siphoned roughly $230 million in ETH from the protocol. More than 95 percent of the unbacked rsETH has been reclaimed, reducing the exposure for lenders. The remaining shortfall will be covered by the DeFi United coalition, helping to stabilize the market and protect institutional participants.

Crypto security is turning into an AI arms race, Elliptic CEO warns

CoinDesk • Security • Score: 9/10

Elliptic CEO Simone Maini warns that AI‑driven financial activity could overwhelm traditional crypto compliance teams, turning security into an automated arms race. The warning highlights growing concerns among institutional investors about the scalability of current anti‑money‑laundering and fraud detection processes in the cryptocurrency market. To address the threat, Elliptic has raised $120 million from investors that include Nasdaq and Deutsche Bank. The new funding will be used to build AI‑powered monitoring tools designed to help institutions keep pace with increasingly sophisticated illicit activity.

클래리티 법안, 무슨 비즈니스를 해야 하는가?

Tiger Research • Regulation • Score: 9/10

The U.S. Senate Banking Committee approved the CLARITY Act, which prohibits interest on passive stablecoin holdings. The bill allows activity‑linked rewards and was crafted with bipartisan support and the Trump administration. Lawmakers expect it to be signed by July, creating a regulatory shift for crypto yield offerings. Institutional investors should anticipate new token‑banking services as platforms adapt to the new framework.

SEC to propose tokenized stock framework as Wall Street efforts deepen: Bloomberg

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 7/10

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is drafting an “innovation exemption” that would let trading platforms list tokenized versions of publicly traded equities with a lighter regulatory regime. The exemption is intended to create a clearer pathway for digital securities while preserving investor protections. Major market participants including the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation, Nasdaq and the New York Stock Exchange are already constructing the technology and settlement infrastructure needed for tokenized stocks and exchange‑traded funds. If adopted, the framework could enable faster settlement, broader market access and new liquidity channels for institutional investors.

Hyperliquid's USDC deal could supercharge HYPE, pressure Circle, Coinbase margins, analysts say

CoinDesk • DeFi • Score: 7/10

Hyperliquid has entered a revenue‑share agreement with Circle and Coinbase that allows the exchange to capture up to 90% of USDC reserve income. The arrangement could channel between $135 million and $160 million each year into Hyperliquid’s ecosystem. USDC is now designated as the platform’s official Aligned Quote Asset, giving Hyperliquid a direct lever to generate buying pressure for its native HYPE token. By diverting a large share of USDC earnings, the deal is expected to compress margins for both Circle and Coinbase, a dynamic that analysts say could reshape competitive dynamics in the stablecoin market.

Solana is shedding its memecoin reputation as big banks move billions into its ecosystem

CoinDesk • Infrastructure • Score: 7/10

Solana is transitioning from a perception centered on meme‑driven tokens to a platform embraced by large financial institutions. According to Messari, major banks and payment firms are routing billions of dollars of tokenized funds, equities and exchange‑traded products onto Solana’s low‑fee, fast‑settlement network. This influx of real‑world asset and stablecoin activity demonstrates that the blockchain is being used for genuine financial infrastructure rather than speculative trading. Institutional investors view Solana’s scalability and cost advantages as a foundation for broader tokenized finance and cross‑border payment solutions.

Strategy (MSTR) news: Company purchased more than $2 billion of BTC last week

CoinDesk • Bitcoin • Score: 7/10

MicroStrategy acquired 24,869 Bitcoin for approximately $2.01 billion, raising its total holdings to 843,738 coins. The average cost basis of the company’s Bitcoin portfolio is now $75,700 per coin. The purchase was funded primarily through the issuance of STRC preferred stock and partially through common stock sales. The transaction occurred as Bitcoin prices slipped modestly and MicroStrategy shares fell about 2.5 percent in pre‑market trading, underscoring the firm’s continued commitment to Bitcoin as a treasury asset.

Ethereum news: Bitmine (BMNR) buys the dip as Tom Lee ties ether's pullback to rising oil prices

CoinDesk • Ethereum • Score: 7/10

Bitmine Immersion, an Ethereum treasury firm, bought 71,672 ETH for roughly $157 million, raising its stake to about 4.37 % of the circulating supply. The purchase represents a sharp increase from the previous week, indicating the firm is actively buying on the recent market dip. Chairman Tom Lee linked the recent ETH pullback to rising oil prices and called the dip an attractive buying opportunity that could precede a price rebound.

Revolut wants to take Dogecoin (DOGE) mainstream with its new physical payment card

CoinDesk • Payments • Score: 6/10

Revolut, a UK‑based fintech, has introduced a physical debit card branded with Dogecoin that enables users to spend the cryptocurrency wherever Visa or Mastercard are accepted. The card performs an automatic conversion of DOGE to fiat at the point of sale and does not charge additional exchange fees. The rollout is being launched in the United Kingdom and the European Union as the first markets for the product. This move illustrates the growing effort by fintech firms to embed crypto assets into mainstream payment flows, a development that may drive higher transaction volumes and attract institutional liquidity providers.

Bitcoin Price Action Wipes All Its May Gains to Return to $76,000

Cointelegraph • Bitcoin • Score: 6/10

Bitcoin fell back to $76,000, wiping out the gains it had accumulated in May. The retreat followed a net outflow of about $1 billion from U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange‑traded funds, indicating reduced institutional appetite. Analysts attribute the decline to weak ETF inflows, broader macroeconomic headwinds, and widening gaps in CME Bitcoin futures contracts. The price movement is closely watched by institutional investors because it reflects the health of the emerging crypto‑ETF market and overall market sentiment.
