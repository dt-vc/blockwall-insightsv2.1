Morgan Stanley Bitcoin ETF gets off to a strong start with $33m debut

DL News • Investment • Score: 7/10

Morgan Stanley launched its inaugural Bitcoin exchange‑traded fund, ticker MSBT, on NYSE Arca. The fund attracted more than $33 million of net inflows on the first trading day, placing it in the top one percent of all ETF launches. With an expense ratio of 0.14 percent, it is currently the lowest‑cost Bitcoin ETF in the United States and could trigger fee‑price competition among existing providers, a factor that may be relevant for institutional allocation decisions.

The Protocol: Bernstein says quantum threat to Bitcoin is real but manageable

CoinDesk • Bitcoin • Score: 6/10

Bernstein, a Wall Street broker, has warned that advances in quantum computing pose a credible but manageable risk to Bitcoin’s cryptographic security. The firm points to recent breakthroughs, including Google’s reduction in the number of qubits needed for certain calculations, which could shorten the timeline for a potential quantum attack. However, Bernstein notes that scaling quantum hardware to the level required to break Bitcoin’s elliptic‑curve encryption remains a complex, long‑term challenge, suggesting the immediate threat is limited.

Stablecoins to transact $1.5 quadrillion? Yes, Chainalysis really just made that forecast

DL News • Stablecoins • Score: 6/10

Chainalysis forecasts that stablecoin transaction volume could reach $1.5 quadrillion by 2035, propelled by an anticipated $100 trillion generational wealth transfer and expanding merchant acceptance. The firm also projects the overall stablecoin market to rise from roughly $317 billion today to about $2 trillion by 2028. If realized, these figures would position stablecoins as a payment infrastructure comparable to Visa and Mastercard, a development that institutional investors should monitor for potential exposure and strategic positioning.

White House study bolsters crypto's stance in stablecoin yield fight against bankers

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 6/10

The White House Council of Economic Advisers released a report that challenges the banking sector’s argument that banning stablecoin yield would protect credit creation. The analysis concludes that prohibiting yield‑like rewards would have little effect on bank lending while removing a source of competitive returns for consumers. The findings strengthen the crypto industry’s position in the Senate’s Clarity Act debate, a key regulatory discussion for institutional investors.

Bitcoin, Ethereum surge after US and Iran agree to ceasefire

DL News • Bitcoin • Score: 6/10

Bitcoin moved above $72,000 and Ethereum climbed past $2,200 following the announcement of a two‑week ceasefire between the United States and Iran. The ceasefire announcement removed a key source of geopolitical risk that had been depressing crypto prices. Market participants attributed the price jump to rapid unwinding of short positions, according to sFOX’s chief business officer. The rally highlights how geopolitical developments can quickly alter risk sentiment and liquidity dynamics for institutional crypto exposure.

Why Coinbase sees the Clarity Act fuelling its EU crypto growth and reshaping regulation

DL News • Regulation • Score: 5/10

Coinbase argues that the U.S. Clarity Act will increase confidence among institutional investors for its operations in the European Union. The legislation provides clearer regulatory guidance on crypto activities, which the exchange believes will facilitate its regional expansion. A key element of the bill is the treatment of stable‑coin yield, which Coinbase says could influence how the EU’s MiCA framework is amended in the future. This potential alignment of U.S. and EU rules is seen as a catalyst for broader institutional participation in crypto markets across Europe.

White House advisers: Clarity Act allowing stablecoin yield won’t harm banks

DL News • Regulation • Score: 5/10

A recent report from the White House Council of Economic Advisers examined the effect of prohibiting yield on stablecoins. The analysis concluded that such a ban would raise bank deposits by only $2.1 billion, representing a 0.02 percent increase, which the advisers deem negligible. These findings underpin the pending Clarity Act that would allow crypto firms to provide stablecoin reward programs. The advisers argue that the legislation would have minimal impact on community banks and should not be a concern for the broader banking sector.

An Interactive Guide to GENIUS Implementation

Paradigm • Regulation • Score: 5/10

The GENIUS Act, the first U.S. legislation to establish a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins, has entered the rulemaking stage. Paradigm has introduced a public GENIUS Act Tracker that follows the progress of the 21 agency rulemakings and associated public comment periods required for the law’s implementation. The tracker gives institutional investors a centralized tool to assess regulatory timelines and compliance obligations as the stablecoin ecosystem evolves.
