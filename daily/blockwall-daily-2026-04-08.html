Morgan Stanley Bitcoin ETF gets off to a strong start with $33m debut

DL News • Investment • Score: 7/10

Morgan Stanley launched its inaugural Bitcoin exchange‑traded fund, ticker MSBT, on NYSE Arca. The fund attracted more than $33 million of net inflows on the first trading day, placing it in the top one percent of all ETF launches by initial capital. With an expense ratio of 0.14 percent, MSBT is the lowest‑cost Bitcoin ETF currently available in the United States. The combination of strong early demand and a competitive fee structure is likely to intensify pricing pressure among existing Bitcoin ETF providers, a development that institutional investors should monitor.

The Protocol: Bernstein says quantum threat to Bitcoin is real but manageable

CoinDesk • Bitcoin • Score: 6/10

Bernstein, a Wall Street brokerage, has warned that advances in quantum computing pose a credible risk to Bitcoin’s cryptographic security. The firm notes that recent breakthroughs, including Google’s reduction in the number of qubits needed for certain calculations, have shortened the projected timeline for a possible quantum attack. However, Bernstein emphasizes that scaling quantum hardware to the level required to break Bitcoin’s elliptic‑curve encryption remains a complex, long‑term challenge and the threat is considered manageable for now.

Stablecoins to transact $1.5 quadrillion? Yes, Chainalysis really just made that forecast

DL News • Stablecoins • Score: 6/10

Chainalysis projects stablecoin transaction volumes could reach $1.5 quadrillion by 2035. The forecast links this growth to a $100 trillion generational wealth transfer and expanding merchant adoption. It suggests stablecoins could become a core payment infrastructure rivaling Visa and Mastercard. The market is expected to expand from $317 billion today to $2 trillion by 2028.

White House study bolsters crypto's stance in stablecoin yield fight against bankers

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 6/10

A report from the White House Council of Economic Advisers challenges the banking sector’s argument that banning stablecoin yield would safeguard credit creation. The analysis finds that prohibiting yield‑like rewards would have little effect on bank lending while removing a source of competitive returns for consumers. The findings strengthen the crypto industry’s stance in the Senate’s Clarity Act debate and signal potential regulatory headwinds for institutions considering stablecoin yield strategies.

Bitcoin, Ethereum surge after US and Iran agree to ceasefire

DL News • Bitcoin • Score: 6/10

Bitcoin rose above $72,000 following the announcement of a two‑week ceasefire between the United States and Iran. Ethereum also moved higher, trading above $2,200. The ceasefire news removed bearish sentiment from the crypto market and prompted a wave of short‑position unwinding. sFOX’s chief business officer highlighted the unwind as a key driver of the rally, underscoring the sensitivity of digital assets to geopolitical developments.

Why Coinbase sees the Clarity Act fuelling its EU crypto growth and reshaping regulation

DL News • Regulation • Score: 5/10

Coinbase says the U.S. Clarity Act will boost its expansion in the EU by giving institutional investors regulatory confidence. The bill’s provisions, especially around stable‑coin yield, could also shape future amendments to the EU’s MiCA framework. This alignment is significant because it may encourage more institutional capital to enter European crypto markets under clearer rules.

White House advisers: Clarity Act allowing stablecoin yield won’t harm banks

DL News • Regulation • Score: 5/10

A recent report from the White House Council of Economic Advisers estimates that prohibiting stablecoin yield would raise bank deposits by only $2.1 billion, roughly 0.02 percent of the banking system. The analysis directly challenges claims from the banking lobby that stablecoin rewards pose a systemic risk. The findings underpin the pending Clarity Act, which would allow crypto firms to continue offering stablecoin incentives with only a minimal effect on community banks, a factor that institutional investors should note when assessing regulatory risk.

An Interactive Guide to GENIUS Implementation

Paradigm • Regulation • Score: 5/10

The GENIUS Act, the first U.S. legislation to establish a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins, has entered the rulemaking stage. This transition triggers a series of 21 agency rulemakings and associated public comment periods that will shape how stablecoins can be issued and used for payments. Paradigm has responded by releasing an interactive GENIUS Act Tracker that publicly aggregates progress across all agencies. Institutional investors can use the tracker to monitor regulatory developments and assess compliance risks and opportunities in the emerging stablecoin market.
