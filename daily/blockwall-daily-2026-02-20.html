Illicit Stablecoin Activity Hit a Five-Year High in 2025

News • Stablecoins • Score: 7/10

Illicit stablecoin activity reached a five-year high in 2025, with wallets linked to illicit entities receiving $141 billion, primarily driven by sanctions evasion networks that accounted for 86% of all illicit crypto flows. Roughly half of this volume, or $72 billion, involved the Russian ruble-pegged A7A5 token, which connects sanctioned actors from Russia, China, Iran, North Korea, and Venezuela, enabling large-scale value movement outside traditional financial controls. For institutional investors, this underscores stablecoins' dual role as core infrastructure for legitimate treasury operations and remittances, exceeding $1 trillion in monthly volumes, while representing just 1% of total flows amid rising compliance risks from concentrated sanctions exposure. TRM Labs emphasizes that 99% of stablecoin activity remains licit, but enhanced monitoring of high-risk networks is critical for risk management.
