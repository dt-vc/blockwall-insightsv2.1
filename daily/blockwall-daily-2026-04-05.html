Drift says $270 million exploit was a six-month North Korean intelligence operation

CoinDesk • Security • Score: 9/10

A North Korean state‑linked group infiltrated the Drift Protocol for six months while posing as a quantitative trading firm. On April 1 the group executed a $270 million exploit that drained funds from the platform. The attackers first built credibility by depositing more than $1 million and then compromised multisig approvals using a malicious TestFlight application and a VSCode/Cursor vulnerability. The incident highlights significant weaknesses in DeFi multisig security models and underscores the need for stronger authentication and code‑review practices for institutional participants.

Why the mind-bending physics of quantum computing is terrifying for bitcoin and crypto

CoinDesk • Security • Score: 6/10

Google’s research shows that a sufficiently advanced quantum computer could theoretically derive a Bitcoin private key from its public key in about nine minutes. This capability would exploit the fundamental difference between quantum bits, which can exist in multiple states simultaneously, and classical bits that are limited to 0 or 1. If realized, such quantum attacks would threaten the cryptographic security underpinning Bitcoin and other blockchain platforms, prompting institutional investors to reassess risk exposure.

Visa says South Korea an optimal testbed for stablecoins amid call for ‘urgent regulation’

DL News • Stablecoins • Score: 5/10

Visa has identified South Korea as an optimal testbed for stablecoins because of the country’s large crypto‑investor base and widespread AI adoption. The payments company is urging Korean regulators to enact clear stablecoin legislation to support the sector’s growth. Domestic financial groups have expressed frustration with regulatory delays and Visa’s remarks suggest a positive outlook for stablecoin experimentation in the Korean market.

Clarity Act could die, expert warns, but crypto lobbyists say bill has ‘meaningful momentum’

DL News • Regulation • Score: 5/10

The Clarity Act, a legislative proposal to codify cryptocurrency regulations in U.S. law, has attracted bipartisan support, according to the Blockchain Association. Legal analysts caution that the bill may lose momentum after the summer because of the political dynamics surrounding the upcoming midterm elections. A senior Coinbase executive anticipates that the Senate Banking Committee will schedule a hearing on the measure in the near term. However, a potential transition to a Democrat‑controlled committee could stall the legislation and impede further development of crypto innovation.
