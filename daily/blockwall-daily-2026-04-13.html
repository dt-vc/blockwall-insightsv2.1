SEC Proposes Certain Crypto Interfaces Don’t Need to Register as Brokers

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 6/10

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has issued a proposal that certain cryptocurrency interfaces would be exempt from broker registration requirements. The proposal distinguishes platforms that only provide technical infrastructure from those that act as brokers. By clarifying the regulatory scope, the SEC aims to reduce compliance burdens for infrastructure providers. This guidance could affect how institutional investors evaluate the regulatory risk of using such platforms.

U.S. lawmakers take another swing at crypto tax policy with revised bill

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 6/10

U.S. lawmakers have re‑introduced the PARITY Act as a revised effort to overhaul the tax treatment of cryptocurrency transactions. The new draft eliminates the $200 de‑minimis threshold for stablecoin trades, establishes a $1 deemed basis for crypto assets, and expands wash‑sale rules to cover digital holdings. By separating passive staking income from active trading, the legislation seeks to provide clearer guidance for institutional investors on reporting and compliance requirements.

A hacker created $1.2bn of counterfeit crypto. They only sold it for $237,000

DL News • Security • Score: 6/10

A code exploit on the Hyperbridge cross‑chain bridge enabled a hacker to mint one billion counterfeit Polkadot DOT tokens on the Ethereum network. The forged tokens were valued at roughly $1.2 billion. The attacker was only able to liquidate about $237 k before the operation was detected. The breach underscores the persistent security vulnerabilities of cross‑chain bridges and the need for robust safeguards to protect institutional exposure.

The geopolitical risk prompted traders to buy protective put options, signaling heightened hedging activity. Additional volatility may arise from central‑bank meetings that could affect monetary‑policy expectations.

Korean crypto ‘revenge agencies’ say they’ll murder people for Tether despite ‘ringleader’ arrest

DL News • Security • Score: 5/10

South Korean police have reported that organized “revenge agencies” are still providing on‑demand intimidation, assault and murder services in exchange for payments in the USDT stablecoin. The criminal groups continue to advertise these illicit services on Telegram and X despite the arrest of a suspected ringleader. They require deposits that range from $300 to $5,700 and claim to record the attacks for their clients. The persistence of such illicit activity highlights regulatory and reputational risks for institutions with exposure to USDT and underscores the need for enhanced AML monitoring.

Coinbase has launched a decentralized exchange portal for UK customers, giving them access to DeFi protocols such as Uniswap and Aerodrome through the Coinbase app. The feature, dubbed a “DeFi mullet,” is part of Coinbase’s broader push to become an “everything exchange.” By embedding DeFi services within a regulated framework, Coinbase aims to demonstrate that decentralized finance can be offered safely to mainstream investors.

Trump-backed World Liberty Financial’s token plummets to all-time low as firm calls self-dealing accusations ‘FUD’

DL News • DeFi • Score: 5/10

World Liberty Financial, a firm backed by former President Trump, saw its WLFI token fall to an all‑time low after it was used as collateral on the Dolomite lending protocol. The collateralization enabled the company to borrow $75 million of stablecoins, including its own USD1 token, creating a circular borrowing structure that left USD1 lenders unable to redeem their holdings. The arrangement has drawn criticism and the company has labeled the self‑dealing accusations as “FUD,” while a vote is scheduled to decide a phased unlock schedule for the token.
