SEC Proposes Certain Crypto Interfaces Don’t Need to Register as Brokers

Cointelegraph • Regulation • Score: 6/10

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has issued a proposal stating that certain cryptocurrency interfaces would not be required to register as brokers. The clarification is intended to reduce compliance burdens for platforms that merely provide technical infrastructure rather than brokerage services. Institutional investors should monitor the proposal because it could affect the regulatory landscape for crypto service providers and the associated risk assessments.

U.S. lawmakers take another swing at crypto tax policy with revised bill

CoinDesk • Regulation • Score: 6/10

U.S. lawmakers have re‑introduced the PARITY Act as a revised effort to overhaul the tax treatment of cryptocurrency transactions. The new draft eliminates the $200 de‑minimis threshold that previously applied to stablecoin trades, meaning all such transactions will be reportable. It also establishes a $1 deemed basis for crypto assets and expands wash‑sale rules to cover crypto while drawing a clear line between passive staking income and active trading gains. The changes are intended to provide greater certainty for institutional investors and simplify compliance with IRS reporting requirements.

A hacker created $1.2bn of counterfeit crypto. They only sold it for $237,000

DL News • Security • Score: 6/10

A code exploit on the Hyperbridge cross‑chain bridge enabled a hacker to mint one billion counterfeit Polkadot DOT tokens on the Ethereum network, representing a theoretical value of $1.2 billion. The attacker was only able to liquidate approximately $237 000 of the counterfeit tokens before the operation was halted. The event underscores the persistent security vulnerabilities of cross‑chain bridges while indicating that the immediate financial impact on the market was relatively contained.

Korean crypto ‘revenge agencies’ say they’ll murder people for Tether despite ‘ringleader’ arrest

DL News • Security • Score: 5/10

South Korean police have reported that organized “revenge agencies” are still providing on‑demand intimidation, assault and murder services in exchange for payments in the USDT stablecoin. The groups continue to operate despite the recent arrest of a suspected ringleader. They promote their illicit offerings on Telegram and X, require deposits between $300 and $5,700, and claim to record the attacks for paying clients. The activity highlights ongoing law‑enforcement and compliance risks associated with the use of stablecoins in illicit markets, a concern for institutional investors monitoring crypto‑related exposure.

Trump-backed World Liberty Financial’s token plummets to all-time low as firm calls self-dealing accusations ‘FUD’

DL News • DeFi • Score: 5/10

World Liberty Financial, a firm backed by former President Donald Trump, saw its WLFI token fall to an all‑time low. The decline followed the company's use of the WLFI token as collateral on the Dolomite lending protocol to secure $75 million of stablecoins, including its own USD1 token. The circular borrowing structure has left USD1 lenders unable to redeem their holdings and has drawn criticism as a potential self‑dealing scheme. In response, the firm announced a forthcoming vote on a phased token unlock schedule to address liquidity concerns.
