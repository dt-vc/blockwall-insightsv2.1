Prediction markets are bending insider trading rules. But will they break them?

Liam Kelly • Regulation • Score: 6/10

Prediction markets, regulated by the CFTC as event contracts, face growing scrutiny over insider trading risks due to unclear definitions of material nonpublic information and the broad scope of available bets. Lawmakers, led by Representative Ritchie Torres, have introduced the Public Integrity in Financial Prediction Markets Act of 2026 to prohibit elected officials from trading contracts tied to administration policies, addressing demonstrated dangers like suspicious Polymarket trades before events such as the Maduro operation. CFTC Chairman Michael Selig signals imminent rulemaking to clarify insider trading rules while asserting exclusive federal jurisdiction against state encroachments, a development vital for institutional investors seeking compliance certainty amid rising retail volumes and platforms like Kalshi, Crypto.com, and Robinhood. No major U.S. arrests have occurred yet, but platforms monitor for manipulation and report to the CFTC, heightening the need for robust surveillance policies.
