Man sentenced to 20 years for role in $74 million crypto investment fraud

Brian Danga • Security • Score: 7/10

Daren Li, a 42-year-old key figure in an international "pig butchering" scam syndicate operating from Cambodia, was sentenced in absentia to 20 years in prison and three years of supervised release for conspiring to launder $73.6 million stolen from U.S. victims through fake cryptocurrency investment schemes. Li pleaded guilty in November 2024 after his arrest in Atlanta but fled in December 2025 by cutting off his ankle monitor, making him the first direct recipient of victim funds among eight co-conspirators to receive such a maximum sentence. The fraud involved building trust via social media, dating apps, and encrypted messages before draining victims' crypto wallets, with laundered funds moved through 74 U.S. shell companies to banks like Deltec in the Bahamas and converted to Tether. Institutional investors should note this U.S. Department of Justice enforcement underscores heightened scrutiny on crypto laundering risks, potentially impacting compliance and due diligence for cross-border transactions.
