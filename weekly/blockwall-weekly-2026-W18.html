🔥 Top Stories of the Week

Crypto industry reels as April sees highest number of hacks ever

DL News • Security • 2026-05-01 • Score: 10/10 Bearish

April recorded a historic 29 crypto hacks, the highest monthly total ever, resulting in a combined loss of $579 million from the Drift exchange and Kelp DAO. The majority of incidents were caused by code bugs and single points of failure, highlighting growing security concerns in DeFi.

Why It Matters
The surge to 29 hacks and $579 million losses underscores heightened security risk, likely compressing DeFi valuations and prompting VCs to prioritize funding for audit, insurance and formal‑verification infrastructure. STORY 2: Aave’s $300 million rescue shows that coordinated liquidity backstops can stabilize DeFi after large exploits, reinforcing Aave’s market position and supporting the $2 trillion tokenised RWA thesis that may attract new institutional capital. STORY 3: The $300 million DeFi United fund demonstrates strong community capital mobilisation, suggesting that future breach mitigation may rely on pooled rescue mechanisms rather than individual protocol balance sheets. STORY 4: The pledge of 30,000 ETH by major Ethereum stakeholders signals that core infrastructure players are willing to inject native assets to protect bridge liquidity, which could set a precedent for on‑chain risk‑sharing arrangements. STORY 5: MegaETH’s 55 percent price collapse highlights the volatility of layer‑2 token launches and suggests that venture capital may become more cautious about funding token‑centric roll‑outs without proven usage metrics. STORY 6: Aave’s ability to weather a $193 million loss and retain ~50 percent of lending market share reinforces its “too‑big‑to‑fail” perception, potentially justifying higher valuations and attracting capital seeking exposure to a de‑facto core protocol. STORY 7: Meta’s integration of USDC on Polygon and Solana for creator payouts validates large‑scale stablecoin adoption on L2 and non‑EVM chains, opening a commercial runway for infrastructure providers and increasing regulatory scrutiny around cross‑border payments. STORY 8: Israel’s issuance of a regulated shekel‑pegged stablecoin on Solana establishes a sovereign‑backed use case for non‑USD stablecoins, encouraging VCs to explore regional fiat‑stablecoin partnerships and highlighting the importance of compliant custodial solutions. STORY 9: Aave’s detailed remediation plan to close a $246 million deficit demonstrates governance agility and could boost confidence in protocol resilience, making Aave a more attractive target for risk‑aware investors. STORY 10: Justin Sun’s $20 million liquidity injection and push for Aave on Tron signals a strategic alliance that could expand Aave’s addressable market and position Tron as a competitive hub for high‑value DeFi protocols.

📡 Notable Signals & Patterns

Record‑high DeFi hack wave underscores security fragility April saw 29 crypto hacks , the most ever recorded in a single month , wiping out $579 million, with the Drift exchange and Kelp DAO among the biggest victims. The incidents were largely traced to code bugs and single points of failure, highlighting that even mature DeFi protocols remain vulnerable. Blockwall should anticipate heightened demand for formal security audits, bug‑bounty platforms and insurance products that can protect DeFi infrastructure investors. 2. Aave‑driven “DeFi United” rescue proves systemic clout and community solidarity Following the Kelp DAO exploit, Aave coordinated a $300 million rescue fund that attracted pledges from multiple DAOs, crypto firms and high‑profile individuals. The coalition also contributed 30,000 ETH to plug a $246 million hole left by the attack, reinforcing Aave’s role as a de‑facto backstop for the lending market. This pattern suggests that large‑scale, protocol‑level safety nets may become a standard expectation for investors in DeFi infrastructure. 3. Non‑USD stablecoins gain regulatory approval and user adoption Israel’s Capital Market Authority approved the first regulated shekel‑pegged stablecoin (BILS) issued on Solana with custodianship by Fireblocks and audit by EY, marking a clear move away from USD‑centric stablecoins. At the same time, Bitso’s 2025 adoption report showed stablecoins capturing 40 % of crypto purchases in Latin America, overtaking Bitcoin’s 18 % share. The twin developments point to a diversification of stablecoin rails that could open new tokenisation opportunities and reduce reliance on US‑dollar pegs. 4. Institutional Bitcoin accumulation and geopolitical positioning reinforce long‑term confidence Strategy (MSTR) added $255 million of Bitcoin, bringing its treasury to 818,334 BTC valued at roughly $61.8 billion, while the U.S. Pentagon publicly confirmed classified Bitcoin projects, framing the asset as a national‑security tool. Despite short‑term price dips below $75 k after the Fed’s rate decision, these actions signal sustained institutional belief in Bitcoin as a store of value and strategic asset, supporting continued capital inflows into Bitcoin‑related infrastructure. 5. Stablecoin‑enabled payments bridge traditional finance and Web3 ecosystems Meta launched creator payouts in Circle’s USDC on Polygon and Solana via Stripe, initially targeting Colombia and the Philippines, and Shinhan Card announced a partnership with Solana to offer stablecoin‑based card payments and DeFi services in Korea. OKX’s open‑standard payment protocol for autonomous AI agents further expands the use‑case spectrum. The convergence of stablecoins, payment processors and legacy finance institutions suggests a rapid scaling of wallet/identity solutions that can capture cross‑border transaction volume.
