📊 Month in Review

May was defined by a single regulatory breakthrough that reshaped the narrative of crypto’s legitimacy. The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission granted the first approval for a Bitcoin perpetual futures contract on a regulated exchange, allowing Kalshi to list BTCPERP and opening a pathway for Coinbase affiliates to route American clients to global options and perps. This move does more than add a new product. it erects a clear, enforceable framework for the most liquid segment of crypto trading and signals that the United States is finally aligning its oversight with market reality. For European builders, the precedent offers a template to secure comparable licences and to attract the institutional demand that now has a regulated home. Capital followed the regulatory signal with unprecedented vigor across Asia and beyond. OKX Ventures and Korea Investment & Securities each placed roughly $53 million into Coinone for a combined 19.6 percent stake, while Samsung’s conglomerate units injected $408 million into Dunamu, the operator of Upbit. Hana Bank added a $670 million, 6.55 percent position in the same exchange, and together these deals represent more than $1 billion of corporate money flowing into South Korean infrastructure. The United States also demonstrated its enforcement muscle by seizing about $1 billion of Iranian crypto assets, a reminder that compliance risk is now a material factor in capital allocation. The scale and diversity of these moves matter because they confirm that sophisticated investors are willing to fund exchanges that can meet emerging regulatory standards, and they provide the liquidity foundation that European platforms can tap into. Infrastructure is being rewired to support that liquidity and to meet the new compliance expectations. Bitcoin miner IREN raised roughly $3 billion to convert its hash power into AI‑driven cloud and hyperscale data‑center capacity, a pivot that will supply the GPU horsepower needed for on‑chain analytics and real‑time risk engines. Elliptic secured $120 million to expand AI‑enhanced monitoring tools, a response to the surge in stablecoin and tokenized‑asset scrutiny. Paxos earned SEC clearance to operate a blockchain‑based central securities depository, promising near‑instant settlement for institutional traders. These developments dovetail with Blockwall’s thesis: DeFi infrastructure such as Rebind, secure wallet layers like Keyring Network, and tokenisation platforms including Protokol and FINEXITY will thrive on AI‑powered compliance and high‑speed settlement, while Spiko and bloXmove can leverage the new data‑center capacity to deliver frictionless cross‑border payments. Looking ahead, the next quarter will be a test of how quickly the regulatory template set in the United States can be replicated in Europe and Asia. Founders should prioritize building regulated perpetual markets, integrating AI compliance stacks, and positioning their protocols for instant settlement, areas where our portfolio companies such as Unstoppable Finance and Masa already have a foothold. Investors must watch for further corporate stakes in Asian exchanges, the SEC’s pending rule changes that could lower capital barriers for mid‑size crypto firms, and the United Kingdom’s expanding sanctions regime that will reshape risk assessments. May has shown that capital, compliance and compute are converging. the teams that align their product roadmaps with this convergence will capture the growth that follows.

🎯 Key Themes of May

Regulatory Gateways Open for Crypto Derivatives and Settlement The CFTC’s historic approval for a Bitcoin perpetual contract on a regulated exchange and Paxos’s SEC clearance to clear U.S. equities on blockchain together signal that the United States is finally building a rule‑based runway for crypto‑linked financial products. Kalshi’s listing of BTCPERP and the Coinbase affiliate routing option give U.S. investors domestic access to high‑liquidity perpetuals that were previously confined to offshore venues. Paxos becoming the first blockchain‑based central securities depository puts a near‑instant settlement model on par with the DTCC, a development that European firms can leverage under MiCA’s framework for tokenised assets. We believe the next three to six months will see a wave of derivative issuers filing for CFTC registration and a surge in demand for compliant settlement layers from both CeFi and DeFi players. Builders should prioritize interoperable APIs that bridge regulated perps with on‑chain liquidity, while investors ought to allocate capital to infrastructure providers that can certify compliance across jurisdictions. 2. Corporate Capital Rush Fuels Exchange Consolidation and Prime Brokerage Expansion Samsung’s $408 million stake in Dunamu, OKX Ventures’ $53 million investment in Coinone and Hana Bank’s $670 million purchase of a 6.55 percent share in the same operator illustrate a coordinated corporate endorsement of Asian exchanges, while Ripple’s $200 million credit facility from Neuberger Berman expands its prime‑brokerage capacity for institutional margin financing. These deals inject over $1.5 billion of strategic capital into exchange and brokerage ecosystems, tightening the link between traditional finance and crypto markets. The influx of corporate cash is reshaping governance structures, accelerating product roll‑outs such as won‑pegged stablecoins and on‑chain yield funds managed by Galaxy Digital. We expect the consolidation trend to intensify, with more conglomerates seeking equity positions to secure data pipelines and cross‑border settlement rights. Investors should target platforms that can demonstrate robust KYC/AML compliance and scalable settlement rails, while builders must design modular architectures that can accommodate deep‑pocket partners without sacrificing decentralisation. 3. Security Breaches Trigger a Shift Toward Low‑Risk, Institutional‑Grade DeFi April’s record 29 hacks, highlighted by the $292 million KelpDAO exploit through a LayerZero bridge misconfiguration and the $76 million Echo Protocol minting attack, exposed the fragility of cross‑chain messaging and admin‑key controls. The $293 million KelpDAO loss and the $230 million rsETH exploit at Aave forced lenders to tighten borrowing limits and prompted a market‑wide outflow from high‑risk protocols. In response, compliance firms such as Elliptic raised $120 million to embed AI‑driven monitoring, signalling an emerging arms race between automated attack tools and defensive analytics. We believe the next half‑year will see institutional capital gravitating toward “boring” DeFi, protocols that prioritize audited bridges, formal verification and insurance‑backed liquidity. Builders should double‑down on provable security models and integrate real‑time AI surveillance, while investors must re‑weight portfolios toward platforms with proven bridge resilience and third‑party audit coverage. 4. Tokenized Securities Infrastructure Gains Momentum Across Europe and Beyond DTCC’s selection of Stellar as the backbone for its tokenised settlement platform, Bullish’s $4.2 billion acquisition of transfer‑agent Equiniti and Saudi Arabia’s $12.5 billion droppRWA initiative together illustrate a global push to bring real‑world assets onto blockchain. The Stellar integration embeds compliance tools such as clawbacks directly into the ledger, enabling on‑chain issuance of U.S. Treasuries and equity indexes that can settle in seconds. Bullish now controls issuer records for a large swathe of listed companies, positioning it as a de‑facto gateway for European firms seeking MiCA‑compliant tokenisation pathways. We anticipate that within six months the first tranche of tokenised real‑estate settlements will go live in Saudi Arabia, while European banks will pilot Stellar‑based settlement pilots under MiCA sandbox provisions. Builders should focus on interoperable token standards that satisfy both regulator and market‑maker requirements, and investors should allocate to infrastructure stacks that can service cross‑border token issuance and custodial services at scale. 5. AI Becomes the New Engine for Crypto Infrastructure and Compliance IREN’s $3 billion convertible note financing to build AI‑focused cloud and hyperscale data centres, Elliptic’s $120 million AI‑driven compliance fund and Circle’s $222 million raise for the Arc blockchain illustrate a convergence of artificial intelligence and crypto‑infrastructure. IREN’s pivot from pure mining to AI‑cloud services signals that compute‑heavy workloads such as large language model training will increasingly rely on crypto‑derived energy and hardware. Elliptic’s AI monitoring tools aim to keep pace with the surge in automated attacks, while Circle’s Arc layer‑1 promises high‑speed, compliant rails for stablecoins and tokenised assets. We believe the next three months will see a wave of AI‑enhanced on‑chain analytics products entering the market, and a corresponding rise in demand for low‑latency, AI‑optimised blockchain nodes. Builders should embed AI inference capabilities into node software and offer APIs for real‑time risk scoring, whereas investors should seek out platforms that can monetize AI compute on‑chain and deliver regulatory‑ready analytics to institutional clients.
