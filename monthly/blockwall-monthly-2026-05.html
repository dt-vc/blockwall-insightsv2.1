📊 Month in Review

May was defined by a single, unmistakable signal: Washington finally moved from the periphery to the centre of crypto markets. The CFTC’s historic approval for a regulated bitcoin perpetual contract on Kalshi broke the long‑standing barrier that kept high‑leverage trading out of the United States. This regulatory foothold does more than legitimize a product. it creates a template for every derivative that will follow and forces the market to reckon with a new baseline of compliance. For a continent that has spent the last two years building bridges between fintech and public policy, the American decision validates the European playbook and accelerates the global race for institutional‑grade infrastructure. Capital followed the regulatory cue with unprecedented vigor. OKX Ventures and Korea Investment each placed roughly $53 million into Coinone, together securing a 19.6 percent stake that positions them as the third‑largest shareholders. Samsung’s conglomerate units pumped $408 million into Dunamu, the operator of Upbit, while Hana Bank added a $670 million tranche for a 6.55 percent holding. Across the Atlantic, SpaceX’s pending Nasdaq debut will expose a public market to a treasury that already contains about $1.45 billion in bitcoin, underscoring the appetite for crypto‑linked balance sheets. The sheer volume, well over $1.5 billion in fresh equity across three Asian exchanges, signals that capital is no longer waiting for permission. it is demanding the scaffolding that regulation now promises. The infrastructure layer is being rewired at a comparable pace. IREN’s $3 billion convertible note round earmarks GPU‑driven AI cloud and hyperscale data‑centre build‑out, a move that will feed the compute demands of both AI and blockchain workloads. Elliptic’s $120 million growth round, led by One Peak and backed by Nasdaq Ventures and Deutsche Bank, expands AI‑enhanced compliance tools that are becoming mandatory as stablecoin and token‑asset activity spikes. Paxos secured full SEC registration to clear U.S. equities on a blockchain ledger, a milestone that brings near‑instant settlement to institutional traders and opens a pathway for European post‑trade innovators. For Blockwall’s portfolio, the implications are direct: Keyring Network can leverage the emerging compliant settlement layer, Protokol and Unstoppable Finance stand to benefit from AI‑driven risk engines, while Spiko, FINEXITY, Masa and bloXmove can embed the new derivative products into cross‑border payment pipelines and tokenisation frameworks. Looking ahead, the next quarter will be a test of execution rather than imagination. Founders must align product roadmaps with the CFTC’s derivative framework, ensuring that custody, margin and reporting meet the nascent U.S. standards while still catering to European users. Investors should monitor the rollout of AI‑powered compliance stacks, as the Elliptic funding round suggests a rapid escalation in regulatory scrutiny that will reward firms with provable AML and KYC capabilities. Finally, the convergence of massive capital inflows into Korean exchanges and the imminent SpaceX IPO indicates that the market is preparing for a liquidity surge. projects that can plug into this flow, through tokenised securities, stablecoin issuance or interoperable wallets, will capture the upside. May has handed us the rulebook. the next three months will reveal who can write the winning chapters.

🎯 Key Themes of May

Regulatory Integration Brings Crypto Derivatives Into Mainstream Finance In May the United States took two landmark steps that bring crypto under the same regulatory umbrella as traditional finance. The CFTC granted the first approval for a Bitcoin perpetual futures contract on a regulated exchange, allowing Kalshi to list BTCPERP and opening a regulated path for high‑leverage products. At the same time the SEC cleared Paxos Securities Settlement Company to operate a blockchain based central securities depository for US equities, putting it on a parity footing with the DTCC. These moves give institutional investors a clear compliance framework, reduce the regulatory arbitrage that has driven activity to offshore venues, and set a template that European regulators under MiCA can emulate. Over the next quarter we expect a surge of US‑based asset managers testing regulated crypto derivatives and blockchain settlement services. Builders should prioritize interoperable clearing and reporting layers that satisfy both CFTC and SEC rules, while investors should allocate capital to firms that can bridge the legacy and digital markets. 2. Asian Conglomerates Flood Crypto Exchanges With Institutional Capital May saw a wave of strategic equity deals that cemented Asian conglomerates as major backers of crypto exchanges. Samsung’s units invested $408 million in Dunamu, the operator of Upbit, while Hana Bank bought a $670 million stake and pledged joint work on a won‑pegged stablecoin. OKX Ventures and Korea Investment & Securities each put $53 million into Coinone, creating a combined $106 million infusion pending approval. These investments come as South Korea rolls out new digital‑asset regulations, giving local exchanges a regulatory runway that European firms lack under MiCA. The capital influx signals that Asian markets will become the primary source of liquidity for spot Bitcoin ETFs and tokenized securities in the next six months. European investors should look for partnership opportunities with these exchanges to access Asian user bases, and builders should design cross‑border compliance layers that respect both Korean and EU standards. 3. AI Infrastructure and Compliance Spark New Arms Race in Crypto AI entered the crypto infrastructure arena with two multi‑billion funding rounds in May. Bitcoin miner IREN raised roughly $3 billion of convertible notes to build AI cloud and hyperscale data centres, earmarking the capital for Nvidia GPUs and Dell servers. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic secured $120 million to expand AI‑driven monitoring tools as regulators tighten scrutiny on stablecoins and tokenized assets. The convergence of AI compute power and automated compliance creates a competitive arms race where firms that can process transaction data at scale will dominate the security market. In the next three to six months we anticipate a wave of AI‑enhanced on‑chain risk engines being adopted by exchanges and prime brokers. Investors should favour platforms that embed AI compliance into their core stack, and builders must embed explainable AI models to satisfy both regulators and institutional clients. 4. DeFi Security Crisis Drives Shift Toward Low‑Risk Finance May’s hack tally hit a record with 29 incidents and losses exceeding $579 million, underscoring a systemic security crisis in DeFi. The most visible attacks were the $292 million KelpDAO exploit enabled by a LayerZero bridge misconfiguration and the $76 million Echo Protocol minting attack on Monad. Aave responded by restoring ether borrowing limits after a $230 million rsETH exploit, while the broader community called for “boring” DeFi that prioritises audited code and robust governance. Institutional lenders are now demanding collateral standards and real‑time risk dashboards comparable to traditional finance. Over the next quarter we expect capital to flow toward protocols that lock down cross‑chain messaging and offer insured yield products. Builders should focus on modular security layers and third‑party audit guarantees, and investors should tilt toward platforms that can demonstrate verifiable risk mitigation and insurance coverage. 5. Tokenization Gains Traction From Wall Street to the Middle East Tokenization moved from pilot projects to large‑scale institutional rollouts in May. The DTCC announced that its tokenized securities platform will run on the Stellar blockchain, embedding compliance tools such as clawbacks for US Treasuries and equity settlements. Bullish’s $4.2 billion acquisition of transfer‑agent Equiniti gave it direct access to issuer records and a regulated pathway to tokenise the entire stock market. In parallel, Saudi Arabia’s droppRWA launched a $12.5 billion plan to bring trillions on‑chain, starting with real‑estate tokenisation slated for late 2026. These developments signal that tokenized assets will become a mainstream asset class, prompting European regulators to adapt MiCA guidelines for on‑chain securities. Investors should allocate to infrastructure providers that support Stellar‑compatible settlement and to tokenisation platforms that can bridge EU and Gulf markets, while builders must ensure that their protocols can handle the compliance demands of both jurisdictions.
